TITLE: Your first salary, EPF and tax basics (source: NCFE handbook 2024)
Your take-home pay is usually less than the offer letter. CTC (cost to company) is the total yearly cost to the employer, including its share of provident fund and benefits. In-hand pay is what remains after deductions such as your PF contribution, professional tax and TDS (tax deducted at source). Form 16 is the certificate showing your salary and tax deducted.

EPF (Employees' Provident Fund): you contribute a part of your basic pay and your employer adds a similar amount. NCFE suggests choosing a pay structure where 12 percent of basic goes to EPF, since the employer matches it, making 24 percent in total. It is run by EPFO and is a retirement saving.

Tax saving, with a warning: NCFE explains that Section 80C allows deductions up to Rs. 1.5 lakh for options such as EPF, PPF, NPS, ELSS and NSC, and Section 80CCD(1B) allows an extra Rs. 50,000 in NPS. These deductions mainly apply under the old tax regime, and tax slabs, limits and regimes change often. Always check incometax.gov.in for current rules. You file an income tax return yearly, and you need a PAN. Freelance or side income may also need a return, depending on the amount.

First-salary steps:
1. Build an emergency fund.
2. Check your employer's health cover and add insurance if needed.
3. Start a small SIP and use any bonus either to pay off loans or to invest.
4. Do not let spending rise every time income rises.
5. Keep documents safe: bank, loan, investment, insurance and tax records, and ID such as Aadhaar and PAN. DigiLocker (digilocker.gov.in) stores them digitally.
