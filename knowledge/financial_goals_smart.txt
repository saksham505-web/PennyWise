TITLE: Needs, wants and SMART financial goals (sources: NCFE handbook 2024, RBI school booklet)
Goals tell you why you are saving. First separate needs from wants. Needs are must-haves such as food, a roof, clothes and medicines. Wants are good to have, such as movies, expensive clothes or jewellery, and they can wait.

Goals by time:
1. Short-term (up to about a year): rent, clearing card bills, a new phone.
2. Medium-term (about 1 to 7 years): buying a car, repaying an education loan, a trip.
3. Long-term (about 7 to 25 years or till retirement): retirement, a house, children's education.

Make goals SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic and Time-bound. Saving for a motorbike is vague. Saving Rs. 50,000 for a 100 cc motorbike within 10 months is SMART, because it tells you the amount and the deadline, and breaks down to Rs. 5,000 a month.

How to plan a goal:
1. Identify the purpose.
2. Prioritise, because money is limited and you cannot do everything at once.
3. Estimate the amount needed.
4. Estimate the time. Example: a Rs. 50,000 phone in one year needs about Rs. 4,166 a month, which would be hard on a Rs. 15,000 salary, so the goal or timeline may need adjusting.
5. Track progress and adjust.

Remember: investing without a purpose or goal often leads to the wrong level of risk, and sometimes people have to sell at a loss when they need the money. Match the product to the goal's timeline: money needed soon should stay safe and easy to withdraw.
