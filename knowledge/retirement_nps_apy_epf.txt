TITLE: Retirement planning: NPS, APY and EPF (source: NCFE handbook 2024)
Starting retirement saving early is powerful because of compounding. Retirement planning has to consider inflation, longer life expectancy, rising medical costs, your retirement age and your expected expenses. Most people work from about 25 to 60 and may live 20 years or more after.

NPS (National Pension System): a voluntary, defined-contribution scheme regulated by PFRDA. According to the NCFE handbook it is open to citizens aged 18 to 70, with a minimum of Rs. 500 per year. Money is invested across equity, corporate debt, government securities and other asset classes, and subscribers can pick life-cycle funds (conservative, moderate, aggressive). At 60, up to 60 percent can be taken as a lump sum and at least 40 percent must buy an annuity (monthly pension). Conditional partial withdrawals up to 25 percent of your contributions are allowed after 3 years. Contributions have tax benefits up to limits that change over time.

APY (Atal Pension Yojana): for citizens aged 18 to 40 with a savings bank account. It guarantees a pension of Rs. 1,000 to Rs. 5,000 per month after age 60, depending on contributions. Joining early is cheaper, for example about Rs. 42 a month at age 18 for a Rs. 1,000 pension. After the subscriber's death the spouse gets the same pension, and later the nominee gets the accumulated corpus.

EPF: you and your employer both contribute from your salary. It is run by EPFO. It gives retirement benefits and pension for organised-sector employees.

Other options include pension plans from insurers, pension mutual funds and annuity plans. Limits and rules in this lesson are from 2024 and can change. Check pfrda.org.in and epfindia.gov.in.
