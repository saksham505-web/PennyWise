TITLE: Risk, return, liquidity and diversification (sources: RBI FAME 2024, NCFE handbook 2024)
Every investment has three key features: return (the profit, as income or capital gain), risk (uncertainty, including the chance of losing money) and liquidity (how quickly you can sell at about the current price). Risk and return go together: a higher expected return means higher risk, including the possibility of losing the original amount. Lower return generally means lower risk.

Do not chase returns. Schemes promising high and quick returns can be risky. Check the background and track record of whoever offers the scheme, read the terms and conditions, and do your own study before being tempted by something too good to be true.

Diversification means not putting all your eggs in one basket. You spread money across different assets, such as bank RDs and FDs, bonds, gold, mutual funds, shares and real estate. Their prices often do not fall together, so a fall in one may be balanced by a rise in another. Diversification reduces risk, but it does not remove it.

Your life stage matters. A young person has time to recover from falls and can usually take more risk for the long term. Someone with family responsibilities needs part of the money easily available for emergencies and near-term needs. Near retirement, people usually prefer safer options for steady income.

Inflation matters too: returns should beat inflation after taxes. Also remember costs: brokerage, commissions, demat charges and taxes reduce your return. Track and review your investments regularly, and avoid common behaviour mistakes such as herd mentality, fear of loss and delaying decisions.
