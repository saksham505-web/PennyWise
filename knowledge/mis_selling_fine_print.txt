TITLE: Mis-selling and reading the fine print (sources: RBI FAME 2024, NCFE handbook 2024)
Mis-selling happens when a bank or agent sells you a product you did not need or ask for, or does not explain its important terms. A typical example: you go to open a fixed deposit and the official pushes an insurance policy too.

RBI's advice:
1. Do not mix savings with insurance. Assess your insurance needs and your investment needs separately.
2. Take an insurance policy or investment product only if you need it. A bank cannot force you to take one.
3. Read application forms carefully before signing and never sign blank forms.
4. If a product was sold without your asking or without explaining its terms, complain to the bank. If the bank's reply does not satisfy you, complain to the RBI Ombudsman, in writing or through the RBI Complaint Management System. Banks must display ombudsman details in their branches.

Fine print: almost every financial advertisement has an asterisk. A very high savings rate may need a high minimum balance. A promise to double your money in ten years at 10 percent might actually be simple interest or just a yield figure, not the full picture. Always find out what the asterisk says.

Caveat emptor means let the buyer beware. Most financial products carry no guarantee, so the buyer must ask questions, compare options, and demand details before buying. As consumer protection grows stronger this matters less, but only if buyers stay alert.
