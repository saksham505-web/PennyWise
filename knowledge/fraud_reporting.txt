TITLE: What to do if you lose money to online fraud and your liability (sources: RBI FAME 2024, NCFE handbook 2024)
Speed matters. Report to your bank immediately, whoever is at fault, because the longer you wait, the bigger the loss.

Steps:
1. Call your bank now (use the number on your card or the official app) and ask to block the card, UPI or net banking. Get an acknowledgement of your complaint.
2. Call 1930, the national cybercrime helpline (24x7), and report at cybercrime.gov.in.
3. Save evidence: screenshots, transaction IDs, numbers and chats.
4. If the bank does not solve it, complain to the RBI Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in.

RBI's customer liability rules, as given in the February 2024 RBI booklet:
1. If the bank is at fault, you have zero liability, even if you did not report.
2. If the fault lies elsewhere in the system (third-party breach) and you report within 3 working days, you have zero liability.
3. If you report after 4 to 7 working days in such a case, your liability per transaction is limited to the transaction value or a cap, whichever is lower: Rs. 5,000 for basic savings accounts and Rs. 10,000 for other savings accounts. Beyond 7 days, the bank's board-approved policy applies.
4. If the loss is due to your negligence, such as sharing your password, PIN or OTP, you bear the loss until you report it to the bank.
5. Once you report, losses that happen afterwards are borne by the bank. The bank should credit the disputed amount within 10 working days and resolve the complaint within 90 days.

Failed ATM transactions: banks should resolve them within T+5 days or pay Rs. 100 per day compensation.

Note: RBI has proposed a new framework for digital fraud with different timelines and compensation, so check RBI's website for the latest rules. If you were tricked into approving a payment yourself, getting money back is harder, so prevention is best.
