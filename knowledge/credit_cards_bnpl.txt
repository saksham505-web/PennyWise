TITLE: Credit cards, debit cards and buy now pay later (sources: NCFE handbook 2024, RBI FAME 2024)
A debit card spends your own money straight from your account, so it works on a buy now, pay now principle. A credit card lets you borrow from the card company up to a limit and pay later, so it works on buy now, pay later.

If you pay the whole bill by the due date, you usually pay no interest. If you pay only the minimum due, the rest carries interest, and credit card interest is often very high, in many cases well above 30 percent a year. Late fees and taxes are added on top. Example: on a Rs. 10,000 bill with only the minimum paid, interest of a few hundred rupees can be added every month, and the debt keeps growing.

Good debt vs bad debt: good debt is borrowing to buy something that creates value or income over time, such as an education loan or home loan. Bad debt is borrowing to buy something that quickly loses value. RBI lists using a credit card irresponsibly to fund personal expenses as bad borrowing.

Safe habits:
1. Pay the full bill on time, not the minimum.
2. Spend only what you could pay for from your own money.
3. Avoid cash withdrawals on a credit card.
4. Keep your use low compared with your limit.
5. Never share your card number, expiry date, CVV, PIN or OTP.

Buy now pay later apps are small short-term loans. They feel painless at checkout, but late fees can be high, missed payments can hurt your credit score, and it is easy to lose track. Read the terms, check that the lender is regulated, and always calculate the total you will pay. "No cost EMI" offers may hide processing fees.

Debt tip: list your debts by interest rate and clear credit card and personal loan debt first.
