TITLE: Saving money: safety, liquidity and emergency funds (sources: RBI FAME 2024, NCFE handbook 2024)
Saving means setting aside part of your income. RBI says to look at three things when choosing where to keep savings: safety, liquidity and return. Safety means how sure you are of getting your money back. Government bonds are the safest, and bank fixed deposits are comparatively safe. Liquidity means how easily you can turn the asset into cash without losing value. Return is the earning, and it depends on the product and its risk. Equity shares may give more return but carry a higher risk of loss.

Good saving rules:
1. Spread your savings across different instruments (diversify).
2. Keep a part in liquid form so you can withdraw when needed.
3. Never put money in very risky or unregulated schemes, since you may lose all of it.
4. Clear high-interest debt first, because it costs more than savings earn.

Why save: for goals, for unexpected emergencies (illness, accident, job loss), and to be able to take chances later. An emergency fund is money for sudden needs. A common rule of thumb is three to six months of essential expenses, but a student can start with one month and grow it.

Tools: A regular savings account pays very low interest, so RBI calls it passive saving. A recurring deposit (RD) takes a fixed amount every month and suits people who save small amounts. A fixed deposit (FD) locks a lump sum for a set period. Closing an FD early usually brings a penalty. An auto-sweep facility moves extra savings-account money into an FD automatically so it earns more.

Automate saving by moving money on the day you get your allowance or salary.
