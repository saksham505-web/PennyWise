TITLE: Loans, EMIs and education loans (sources: RBI school booklet 2018, NCFE handbook 2024)
A loan is borrowed money you repay with interest. You repay through EMIs (equated monthly instalments), each containing part principal and part interest. A longer period means a smaller EMI but more total interest. Example: Rs. 5,00,000 at 10 percent a year for 5 years has an EMI of about Rs. 10,624, so you pay about Rs. 6,37,400 in total and roughly Rs. 1,37,400 of it is interest.

Education loans help students fund higher studies; you repay after finishing the course and starting to earn. Vidya Lakshmi (vidyalakshmi.co.in) is a government web portal for students. It shows education loan schemes of many banks and has one common application form, so you register, fill one form and apply to multiple banks, then track your application online.

Figures from RBI's 2018 booklet, based on the Indian Banks' Association Model Education Loan Scheme 2015: maximum loan Rs. 10 lakh for studies in India and Rs. 20 lakh for study abroad; no margin or security needed for loans up to Rs. 4 lakh; EMIs for up to 15 years; and repayment starting after the course with a one-year moratorium. These numbers are from 2018, and banks and government schemes change terms, so check the current conditions before applying.

Tips: borrow only what you need, compare interest rates and fees across banks, find out whether interest builds up during the moratorium, and make sure you can afford the EMI comfortably from your expected salary. Borrow only from regulated lenders. Loans from unregulated entities should be avoided at all costs. If you get a bonus or raise, using it to pay extra EMIs reduces interest.
