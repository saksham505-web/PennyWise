TITLE: Other investment options: PPF, NSC, post office schemes, gold and more (source: NCFE handbook 2024)
Besides shares and mutual funds, India has other options, each with its own risk, return and liquidity.

Government savings schemes: post office savings schemes and recurring deposits, the Public Provident Fund (PPF), National Savings Certificates (NSC) and Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP). They suit people who want safe and fixed returns. Their interest rates are notified every quarter with the approval of the Ministry of Finance, so they can change. Post office schemes carry a sovereign guarantee on the whole amount, so they are considered highly safe. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana is a government deposit scheme for a girl child's education and marriage expenses.

Gold: Sovereign Gold Bonds are government securities denominated in grams of gold, issued by RBI on behalf of the government, as a substitute for holding physical gold. Availability of new issues can change, so check. A Gold ETF is a fund that invests in gold and is traded on the stock exchange.

REITs and InvITs are collective investment vehicles that own real estate and infrastructure and let investors earn from them without buying or managing property.

Debt securities such as bonds are loans to a company or government that pay interest.

ELSS mutual funds are equity funds that also qualify for a tax deduction under the old tax regime.

How to choose: match the product to your goal, time period and risk comfort (see the lessons on risk and goals). Check that the product is regulated and read all terms. Tax rules and rates change, so verify details on the official websites.
