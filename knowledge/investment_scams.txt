TITLE: How to spot investment scams, fake finfluencers and fraud schemes (sources: RBI FAME 2024, NCFE handbook 2024)
People lose money to schemes that look attractive. Know the common types:
1. Ponzi scheme: promoters pay early investors from the money of new investors. When new money stops, the scheme collapses.
2. Pyramid or multi-level marketing: you are asked to join, buy products and recruit others. Eventually recruiting stops and promoters vanish.
3. Lottery or prize scam: you are told you won money but must pay a fee or tax first. Genuine contests never charge a fee to give a prize.
4. Affinity fraud: scammers use a shared community, religion or cause to win trust and ask you to keep it quiet.
5. Fake investment tips and apps through social media, calls and groups.
6. Phishing: fake emails, SMS or websites that steal passwords, card details and PINs.

Red flags: assured or guaranteed returns, "120 percent returns, zero risk", pressure to decide now, secrecy, and loans offered at lower rates than banks (they may take processing fees and vanish).

How to stay safe (RBI advice): deposit or invest only with entities registered with or regulated by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA or the Government. Check the background of the entity. Read the fine print, since an asterisk often hides conditions. RBI and banks never ask for your account, card, PIN or OTP details through email, SMS or calls. RBI does not open accounts for individuals or give cards. RBI's awareness SMS comes from the ID RBISAY, and its helpline for this campaign is 14440.

If cheated: report at once to your bank, call 1930, and report to cybercrime.gov.in. You can report entities that defaulted on deposits at the RBI Sachet portal (sachet.rbi.org.in). Securities-related complaints go to SEBI SCORES.
