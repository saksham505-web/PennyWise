TITLE: Deposit insurance: how DICGC protects your bank money (sources: RBI/DICGC booklet 'The Safety Net', RBI FAME 2024, NCFE handbook 2024)
DICGC (Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation) is a wholly owned subsidiary of RBI. It insures bank deposits. Each depositor in a bank is insured up to a maximum of Rs. 5 lakh, covering both principal and interest, held in the same right and same capacity.

Key facts:
1. You do not pay anything. Banks pay the premium to DICGC. You are insured automatically if your bank is covered.
2. All RBI-licensed banks must have deposit insurance. It includes commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, regional rural banks and cooperative banks registered with DICGC.
3. All types of deposit are covered: savings, fixed, recurring and current. Deposits of governments, other banks and deposits received outside India are excluded.
4. Check whether your bank is insured on dicgc.org.in. Bank websites also show the DICGC logo and QR code.

Same right and same capacity: if you have several accounts in the same capacity, for example savings and fixed deposits as the single holder, they are added together and the limit is Rs. 5 lakh. Accounts in different capacities, such as an individual account, a joint account, a minor's account or an account of a legal entity, each get a separate Rs. 5 lakh cover.

If RBI restricts withdrawals (All Inclusive Directions, or AID): depositors get the insured amount, up to Rs. 5 lakh, within 90 days of the restriction. You submit a claim willingness form, KYC details and an alternate bank account, at the bank or on DICGC's claim willingness portal.

If the bank is liquidated (licence cancelled): you submit KYC and an alternate account to the liquidator, and DICGC pays within two months of receiving the claim list.

Track claims on the Daava Soochak portal, and keep your phone number updated at your bank, since you get SMS updates.
