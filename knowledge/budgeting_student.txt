TITLE: How to budget as a college student (sources: RBI FAME 2024, NCFE handbook 2024, RBI school booklet)
A budget is a plan of your future income and expenses. It can be weekly, monthly or yearly. Comparing it with what you actually spend shows where you overspent, so you can control your money better.

Steps:
1. Write down your income: allowance, stipend, part-time pay.
2. Track every expense for about three months. Keep receipts, note each expense daily and total it at month end. Most people are surprised by what they find. Your UPI history helps.
3. Separate needs (food, rent, transport, medicines) from wants (movies, expensive gadgets, shopping). Wants can be postponed, and learning to postpone them is how you reach your goals.
4. Compare income and expenses. If income is more, you have a surplus: clear any debt first, then save or invest it. If expenses are more, you have a deficit: cut spending or raise income.

The key habit is paying yourself first: set aside savings the moment money arrives, before you spend. Saving equals income minus expenditure, so decide the saving first.

Example: income Rs. 10,000 and expenses Rs. 8,000 leaves Rs. 2,000 to save. If expenses are Rs. 12,000, you are Rs. 2,000 short and will have to borrow, which is a warning sign. A popular rule of thumb, 50/30/20, suggests about half for needs, 30 percent for wants and 20 percent for savings, but it is only a guide.

Budgeting is not one-time. Do it weekly at first, then monthly. Borrowing from friends, family or lenders to cover regular expenses is a sign the budget needs fixing.
